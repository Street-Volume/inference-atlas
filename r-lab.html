Building and comparing discrete distributions

From Project 2 (M463): build a user-defined discrete distribution from scratch — a pmf, its cdf, and a check that probabilities sum to one — then compare it against two "natural" distributions, Binomial and Poisson. This page reimplements the R assignment directly in the browser (no R installed here, no build step) so you can drag the same sliders the R script hard-codes and watch the pmf and cdf redraw live.

R calls, translated
XX <- 1:10; PP <- c(...)
Defining a custom discrete random variable: the support \(x=1,\dots,10\) and its probabilities \(\class{tb}{p(x)}\) — exactly the eleven sliders below (including \(x=0\), set to 0 by default to match the original).
sum(PP)
Task 1(c)'s check that \(\class{tb}{\sum p(x)=1}\). Below, your raw slider values are automatically renormalized so this is always true — drag any slider and watch every bar rescale.
QQ <- cumsum(PP)
The cdf: \(\class{tb}{F(x)=\sum_{k\le x}p(k)}\), plotted as a staircase (R's type="s") in the second panel.
dbinom(x, size=n, prob=p) / dpois(x, lambda)
The Binomial and Poisson pmfs from Project 2, Task 2 — \(\class{tb}{\binom{n}{x}p^xq^{n-x}}\) and \(\class{tb}{\lambda^xe^{-\lambda}/x!}\) — toggle them on to overlay against your custom distribution.
Why compare to Binomial and Poisson specificallyProject 2 picks Binomial(10, 0.6) and Poisson(6) on purpose: the Poisson's λ equals the binomial's mean \(np=10(0.6)=6\). As \(n\to\infty\) and \(p\to0\) with \(np\) held fixed, Binomial\((n,p)\)→Poisson\((np)\) — see the distribution encyclopedia (opens on the Binomial entry) and its limit demo. With \(n\) only 10, the two shapes here are still visibly different (the Poisson's tail reaches past 10, the binomial's can't) — a concrete look at a limit that hasn't kicked in yet.
