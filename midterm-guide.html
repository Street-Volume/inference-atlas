M466 Midterm Study Guide: Modules 1–8

Wackerly, Mendenhall & Scheaffer, Modules 1–8. Midterm window: 3.5 hours once started, open note. Course-wide roadmap — this page is strategy and priority, not new worked problems. Where a listed homework problem already has a full derivation on this site, it's linked.

Most important prep principleTreat every assigned homework problem as an exam prototype. Expect one or two questions to be recognizable homework structures with a changed distribution, parameter, numerical value, target, or an added justification step that tests deeper understanding.

1. Midterm scope and priority map

The natural study boundary is Modules 1–8: probability/distribution review; Chapters 8.1–8.9; 9.1–9.7; 10.1–10.5. Later material (p-values as a separate section, small-sample tests, Neyman–Pearson, likelihood-ratio tests, regression, ANOVA, categorical data) comes after the midterm window.

2. Concept roadmap: how the topics fit together
Point estimation
Asks for a statistic \(T(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)\) estimating \(\theta\). First check bias and MSE; among unbiased estimators, compare variances/relative efficiency.
Consistency
An asymptotic requirement — the estimator should concentrate around the target as \(n\) grows. Common route: show \(E(T_n)=\theta\) and \(Var(T_n)\to0\), then Chebyshev's inequality (Theorem 9.1).
Sufficiency
Asks whether a statistic retains all sample information about \(\theta\). The factorization criterion is the standard tool — see the MVUE guide for the full chain through to completeness.
Rao–Blackwell
Conditioning an unbiased estimator on a sufficient statistic cannot increase variance — the bridge from sufficiency to MVUE construction.
MOM and MLE
Systematic ways to create estimators. MOM equates sample and population moments; MLE maximizes the likelihood over the parameter space — see Part B reading list.
Confidence intervals and tests
CIs invert a pivotal probability statement. Tests formalize decisions with Type I error α and Type II error β. For standard two-sided procedures, the CI and the test give the same reject/do-not-reject conclusion.

4. How to solve the most likely "tweaked HW" questions
A. Pivot → confidence interval
1) Write the exact distribution of the pivot, including degrees of freedom. 2) Put the middle probability \(1-\alpha\) between the correct quantiles. 3) Algebraically isolate the parameter. 4) Check endpoint order after inversion. 5) Substitute data only after the symbolic interval is correct. Common trap: for χ² pivots with the unknown parameter in the denominator, the lower parameter endpoint uses the larger χ² critical value.
B. Prove consistency
Compute \(E(T_n)\) and \(Var(T_n)\). If \(E(T_n)=\theta\) (or tends to θ) and \(Var(T_n)\to0\), write a short Chebyshev argument: \(P(|T_n-\theta|\ge\varepsilon)\le Var(T_n)/\varepsilon^2\to0\) (adjust for bias if needed). For a transformed target \(g(\theta)\), first show \(T_n\to\theta\) in probability, then use continuity of \(g\) when justified.
C. Find a sufficient statistic
Write the joint density/product. Separate it into a θ-dependent factor involving the sample only through \(T(y)\) and a θ-free factor \(h(y)\). Don't omit support indicators if the support depends on θ. For exponential-family-looking densities, collect sums like \(\Sigma Y_i\), \(\Sigma Y_i^2\), or \(\Sigma\log Y_i\).
D. Find an MLE
Write \(L(\theta)\), then \(\ell(\theta)\). Differentiate, solve the score equation, verify the candidate is a maximum and lies in the parameter space. If the support depends on θ (uniform-type models), the maximum may occur at a boundary — calculus alone can miss it. Normal model warning: with μ unknown, \(\hat\mu_{MLE}=\bar y\) and \(\hat\sigma^2_{MLE}=(1/n)\Sigma(Y_i-\bar y)^2\), which differs from the unbiased \(S^2\) with divisor \(n-1\).
E. Compute α, β, and power
α: evaluate the rejection probability under H₀; choose critical constants from that equation. \(\beta(\theta_1)\): express the acceptance/nonrejection event in terms of the statistic, but evaluate its distribution under \(\theta=\theta_1\), not under \(\theta_0\). If comparing two tests at the same α, compute or reason about power at relevant alternatives — same size does not mean same power.

7. Open-note packet: what to have ready

GoalOpen-note does not mean there is time to search. Build a compact packet that lets you locate a theorem or critical formula in seconds.
  1. Page 1 — distribution facts you actually use
    Normal, t, χ², exponential/gamma, uniform, Rayleigh transformation Y², plus means/variances and key sampling distributions.
  2. Page 2 — estimation
    Bias/MSE, relative efficiency, consistency proof template, factorization criterion, Rao–Blackwell, MOM, MLE checklist.
  3. Page 3 — confidence intervals
    Large-sample mean/proportion/differences, t, χ² variance, exponential χ² pivot, sample-size formulas.
  4. Page 4 — testing
    Hypotheses, α/β/power, Z rejection regions, β/sample-size workflow, CI/test duality.
  5. After those four pages
    Your worked HW solutions M3–M7, each labeled by problem number and one-line "method." Put the problem number in a visible margin/tab so you can jump directly to it.
9. High-frequency mistakes to eliminate
t vs. z
Using z when the exact small-sample normal-mean problem calls for t.
n vs. n − 1
Using n instead of n − 1 in S², or n − 1 instead of n for the normal variance MLE.
χ² endpoints
Reversing χ² endpoints when isolating σ² or θ from a denominator pivot.
"Efficient" without a comparison class
Calling an estimator "efficient" without first being clear about the comparison class (often: unbiased estimators).
Unbiased ⇏ consistent
Claiming consistency only because an estimator is unbiased. Unbiasedness alone does not imply consistency.
Dropping the support
Forgetting the support/indicator in likelihood factorization, especially when the support depends on θ.
Declaring an MLE too early
Solving \(\ell'(\theta)=0\) and declaring an MLE without checking it's a maximum, a boundary, or even in the parameter space.
β under the wrong hypothesis
Computing β under H₀ instead of under the specified alternative.
Misusing p̂ in sample-size planning
Using p̂ inside a sample-size planning formula when the problem says no information about p is available — use .5 for the conservative binomial choice.
Numerical answer only
The exam explicitly requires work for full credit — show the distribution/pivot/test statistic and substitution, not just a final number.
