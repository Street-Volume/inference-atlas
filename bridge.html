The probability function, read backwards

In Part A

You evaluated \(p(y)\) or \(f(y)\) with the parameter known: Poisson(0.5) in Q7, the negative binomial in Q8, \(f(y)=y/2\) in Q9. The parameter was fixed and \(y\) varied.

Q7Q8Q9
In Part B

Fix the observed data and let the parameter vary. The same product of densities is now the likelihood \(L(\theta)\), and the MLE is where it peaks.

9.82(b)9.96Live figure B1
The tool that carries over

\(L(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^n f(y_i\mid\theta)\)

Expected values decide unbiasedness

In Part A

Expectations are sums or integrals weighted by the probability function. Q3 got \(E(Y)=m'(0)=19/9\); Q6 used \(E(aY+b)=aE(Y)+b\).

Q3Q6
In Part B

9.82(c) needs \(E(Y^r)=\theta\), an integral finished with the gamma function, to show \(E(\hat\theta)=\theta\). Unbiased plus a function of the sufficient statistic gives the MVUE.

9.82(c)Live figure B2
The tool that carries over

\(E(\hat\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nE(Y_i^r)=\frac1n(n\theta)=\theta\)

A variance bound becomes a guarantee about estimators

In Part A

From a mean and variance alone, Tchebysheff bounds how likely a variable is to land far from its mean (Q4 to Q6).

Q4Q6Live figure A4
In Part B

Apply it to an estimator. In 9.82, \(Y^r\) is exponential with mean θ, so \(V(\hat\theta)=\theta^2/n\to0\) and the bound forces \(\hat\theta\) toward θ. That property is consistency. It goes past the assigned questions; Wackerly covers it in Section 9.3 as far as I know, so verify the section.

Live figure B2
The tool that carries over

\(P(|\hat\theta-\theta|\ge\varepsilon)\le\dfrac{V(\hat\theta)}{\varepsilon^2}=\dfrac{\theta^2}{n\varepsilon^2}\)

α is an area under a density

In Part A

\(P(a\le Y\le b)=F(b)-F(a)\) is an area under \(f\). Q10(c) computed an upper tail as \(1-F(2)\).

Q9Q10Live figure A7
In Part B

α is the probability the test statistic lands in the rejection region when \(H_0\) is true: the same upper-tail area, computed under the null density.

10.5Live figure B6
The tool that carries over

\(\alpha=P(U\gt c\mid H_0)=1-F_U(c)\)

The distribution of a sum sets the cutoff

In Part A territory

The sum of two independent U(0,1) variables has a triangular density (Example 6.3). It isn't one of the Homework 07 questions, but it uses the same CDF-from-area reasoning as Q9 to Q11.

Q9Q11
In Part B

Test 2's statistic is exactly that sum, so its null distribution is the triangle, and \(c\) comes from solving its CDF. Live figure B7 shows the same event as a corner of the unit square.

10.5Live figure B7
The tool that carries over

\(f_U(u)=u\) on \([0,1]\), \(\ 2-u\) on \((1,2]\), so \(1-F_U(c)=\tfrac12(2-c)^2\) for \(c\in[1,2]\)
